**Figure 1 | Using AI in microscopy image analysis.** **a**, Examples of deep-learning tasks. Models can turn images into measurements (detection, segmentation, classification) or predict image content (denoising, artificial labelling, super-resolution). In predicted images, the content is inferred by the model rather than acquired. **b**, Four ways to use a large language model (LLM), numbered from the least to the most analysis delegated to the model. Each card shows an example prompt and an illustrative reply. The more analysis is delegated, the more decisions are hidden. For example, repeated direct answers can disagree on whether to count a nucleus cut by the image border. **c**, Starting an image-analysis project: (1) define the measurement from the biological question; (2) annotate representative reference images (blue dashed outlines); (3) compare candidate approaches on these images and optimise the most suitable one (magenta outlines; bars show the object-level F1 score at IoU ≥ 0.5); (4) check the final workflow on images not used to make these decisions (here, a second field of the same experiment; custom StarDist F1 0.89 over that field); and (5) use the validated segmentation to track the cells.
