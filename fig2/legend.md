**Figure 2 | Is the deep-learning output good enough for the measurement?** **a**, When a pre-trained model performs poorly, find the mismatch before fine-tuning. A pre-trained generalist model (Cellpose-SAM, default weights) segmenting cancer cells on an endothelial monolayer (brightfield), with one setting changed at a time: (1) the normalisation, (2) the object scale and (3) the post-processing. Each tile shows one variant (magenta, model; dashed, manual annotation); bars give the object-level F1 score against the manual annotation for each variant (IoU ≥ 0.5, whole field, 124 cells; grey, default settings). Forgetting to normalise leaves the model with no detections, and the choice of normalisation and the object scale change the result. At default settings most errors are objects on the endothelium, which threshold changes do not remove; removing low-contrast objects raises F1 from 0.78 to 0.92. (4) Fine-tune, or train a model, only if nothing else works. **b**, For predicted images, first decide what you will measure: an intermediate step is validated through its downstream output; a measurement taken from the predicted image itself is validated against acquired data. **c**, Denoising (CARE). Nuclei segmented on the restored image are measured in the acquired image: the restored image preserves the ranking of nuclear intensities (r = 0.97, per nucleus) but rescales them (median ratio after background subtraction, ×1.55). Pixel-wise agreement is not shown because no matched high-SNR acquisition of this field was available; against the noisy acquired image it would mainly measure the removed noise. **d**, Artificial labelling (pix2pix: DAPI and PECAM-1 predicted from brightfield; the yellow box marks the region shown, predicted and acquired). Intermediate step: cells (Cellpose, predicted PECAM-1) and nuclei (StarDist, predicted DAPI) segmented on the predicted image, compared with manual annotations of the acquired image (object F1 over the whole field; grey, agreement between two annotators). Measurement itself: background-subtracted mean intensity per nucleus (DAPI, manual nuclei) and along the junctions of each annotated cell (PECAM-1), predicted against acquired (Pearson r; one field; a biological claim needs independent paired samples across conditions). **e**, Super-resolution (RCAN; endoplasmic reticulum, BioSR; 31.3 nm pixels; one region of one cell). Left, SQUIRREL (NanoPyx) error map of the prediction against the diffraction-limited input, and the resolution-scaled Pearson correlation (RSP) with that input: the prediction is more consistent with the input than the higher-resolution image is, so consistency with the input does not show that fine structure is genuine. Right, comparison with higher-resolution data (higher-res). Line profile along the dashed line (4.4 µm; a 3-pixel-wide row whose predicted–reference correlation is close to the median over rows). Tubule width: full width at half maximum perpendicular to the tubule, median and interquartile range at the same 2,450 locations in all three images (skeleton points of both the prediction and the reference); the prediction makes tubules narrower than the higher-resolution image (paired median difference −13 nm), beyond the variation between neighbouring points of the higher-resolution image itself (median absolute difference 14 nm versus 23 nm). Predicted images are inferred by the model and are labelled as predicted. Scale bars as indicated.
